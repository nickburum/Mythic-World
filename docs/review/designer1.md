# Designer 1 report

Reviewed commit d95059d in a private worktree, which has now been removed. I played every game in headless Chromium: real taps, watching the autopilot, and the game-over cards. I also ran 300 simulated runs per skill tier against each game's core logic (novice, mid and expert, modelled by reaction delay, timing error and lapse rate). The simulation scripts are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-Mythic-World/b541b170-0079-5108-9be4-0ddb9d42a727/scratchpad/d1/` (sim.mjs, sim2.mjs, play.cjs, play2.cjs) and the screenshot sheet is `d1/sheet.png`.

## Rank the five games best→worst with one line each
1. **Sky Temple**: a proven stacking loop that reads in one second, and regrowing the tower after a run of perfects gives a real comeback. Runs never differ from each other except in colour.
2. **MELT**: the most original verb and the deepest game, since you count taps around the ice → water → steam cycle. Its near-miss reward never fires in normal play, and first runs are harsh.
3. **SKIP**: the best first impression. A run is decided by lily pads, which players don't know they can steer around, and nothing new happens after 320 m.
4. **ORBIT**: dodging is tense, but the score is just survival time and the gems make no difference.
5. **POP (weakest; fix it, don't replace it)**: there is almost nothing to do for the first 30 seconds and no planning at all. With a chain mechanic it fits the chart's match/sort genre instead of being whack-a-mole.

## Findings (ordered by impact; max 12)
- [P0] **POP's first 30 s are mostly waiting.** A bot that tapped every target bubble the moment it appeared scored only 12 in 30.6 s without losing a life, and most of the time there was no target bubble on screen. That rate (about 0.43 targets/s) follows from the tuning: SPAWN_START 1.05 s and TARGET_SHARE 0.45. The first colour change arrives around 25 s. — POP — Set SPAWN_START to 0.6. Make TARGET_SHARE 0.65 for the first 10 pops, easing to 0.40 by score 30. Spawn in waves of 3–5 bubbles with at least 2 targets per wave. Ramp TARGET_EVERY from 6 up to 12 so the first colour change lands by about 8 s.
- [P0] **POP has no planning, cascade or risk choice. This is the box's weakest loop, and the single best addition is chain pop.** — POP — Tapping a target bubble also pops every target-coloured bubble within 80 px of it, and that repeats from each popped bubble. Chain link *n* scores *n* points, so 1+2+3…. Make same-colour bubbles drift toward the nearest same-colour bubble within 120 px at 20 px/s, so clusters form as they rise. The player then chooses between popping now (safe) and waiting for a bigger cluster near the top, which risks an escape and a lost life. That gives a near-miss, a risk/reward call, a high skill ceiling and a screen-wide chain moment. It also justifies the combo counter, which another reviewer called decorative.
- [P1] **ORBIT's gems are decorative, so the score is just survival time.** A simulated player who reversed to chase gems collected 43.4 per minute; one who only dodged collected 43.1. The score works out to about 0.72 × seconds survived. — ORBIT — Add a **momentum multiplier**. Each gem collected without reversing raises the multiplier by 1 (×1 up to ×8), each gem scores the current multiplier, and any reversal resets it to ×1. Spawn 60% of gems on the half of the ring opposite the comet. Every tap now costs something, and holding your line while a warning closes in becomes the near-miss.
- [P1] **MELT's close-call reward never fires.** There were 0 close calls across 900 simulated runs at 0.2–0.38 s reaction time. A close call needs a state change within 0.32 s of reaching the obstacle, and the only reward is +2 XP. Safe early tapping always wins, so the genre's near-miss is missing. — MELT — Add a **heat multiplier**. Each close-call pass adds +1 (up to ×5) and passes score the multiplier. A state change made more than 0.8 s before an obstacle takes 1 off. Show it as a flame meter in the HUD and raise NEAR_MISS_WINDOW to 0.4.
- [P1] **SKIP has no late-run escalation.** DAY_LENGTH is 320 m and the last milestone is at 300 m, so nothing changes after that. The title-screen demo (autopilot) reached 1,960 m in 40 s without sinking. Simulated experts ran 95 s at the median and 330 s at the 90th percentile, against the market's 30–90 s target. — SKIP — Shrink PAD_GAP from 18–34 m to 7–14 m between 55 m and 400 m. From 300 m, pads drift at ±0.6 m/s. From 500 m, pads come in clustered "reed rows" that need two dips in a row. Add a milestone every 250 m after 300 m.
- [P1] **SKIP runs end on a lily pad, and players aren't shown how to avoid it.** All 4 of my real runs with perfect timing ended on a pad, at 338–622 m and 12–20 s each. In simulation, 96% of mid-tier runs ended on a pad. The way out (tapping 0.24–0.45 s early to "dip" the stone and land short) is never taught or signalled. — SKIP — When the predicted landing is on a pad, turn the dashed marker red and pulse "DIP!" once the stone is 0.45 s from the water. Show a coach mark on the first 3 pads of the player's lifetime.
- [P1] **Sky Temple's runs don't differ, and nothing peaks mid-run except the milestone banners.** — Sky Temple — Add **offering stones**: a golden stone every 8–12 floors. A perfect drop scores +5, restores the full footprint and adds a visible temple piece to the tower (a column, then an arch). A non-perfect drop counts as a normal stone. This gives planned peaks, a reason to want a perfect at a particular moment, and a "missed the offering by a hair" near-miss.
- [P1] **The game-over cards don't frame how close the player came, which is the usual "one more run" trigger.** Only MELT gives a line about the death (it explains what went wrong). — All five games — Under the score, add one line, for example "3 short of your best" or "2 floors to Realm of Birds", plus a thin bar showing the run against the player's best. This is separate from Designer 2's XP layer.
- [P1] **MELT's description says a different control scheme from the game.** `melt/index.html` og:description and the store copy in DESIGN.md say "Hold to heat. Release to cool.", but the game is tap to cycle states. — MELT — Rewrite both as "Tap to change state: ice, water, steam." before submission.
- [P2] **MELT's novice runs are very short.** In simulation, novices die after 2 obstacles (about 5 s) in 10% of runs, with a median of 19, before the obstacle labels have finished teaching. — MELT — For a player's first 3 runs ever, use DIFFICULTY_START 2.2 for the first 8 obstacles. A death before score 3 offers a free "try again from here", with no ad.
- [P2] **SKIP is the only game where a first tap on the title screen doesn't start play.** The title is a menu of five stacked buttons, and my tap on the lake landed on Settings. — SKIP — Make a tap anywhere on the lake start a run. Collapse Leaderboard, Challenge, How to play and Settings into a row of icons.
- [P2] **POP's target colour is shown only as a small word under the score** (for example "POP SKY"), while 55% of bubbles are other colours. — POP — Put a large swatch of the target colour in the HUD. Desaturate non-target bubbles by about 40% and drain the colour from the background toward the target hue, so the rule reads without any text.

## Ship verdict
NOT YET — POP is mostly waiting for its first 30 s with no decision depth, and ORBIT's and MELT's scoring ignores the risky play that should make them compelling. The POP density and chain-pop spec plus the ORBIT and MELT multipliers are each a small change to the game's core logic and should land before launch.
