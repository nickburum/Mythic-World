## What the winners share

### Art
- **One palette, high contrast, rounded everything.** Block Blast: 3D-looking blocks with subtle shading, "clean, modern, vibrant colours, smooth animations", tactile feel. Nothing flat-grey; nothing busy.
- **Readable at thumbnail size.** The first App Store screenshot is seen next to the icon in search; the icon and the first frame carry the install.
- **Physical metaphors instead of text.** Royal Match "conveys resource flow through physical, animated metaphors", so there are almost no text boxes. Rewards fly into counters; counters bounce.
- **Character reactions.** A face, a wobble, a cheer: Royal Match's king, Meowdoku's cats. The player is never alone on screen.

### Mechanics and loop
- **Instant entry, staged depth** ("hybrid-casual"): the first 30 seconds need zero instruction; depth arrives through unlocks, not rules.
- **Chain reactions and escalation inside a run**: combos, cascades, special effects "further accelerating the pace".
- **Near-miss death and instant retry** remain the hyper-casual core, now wrapped in a meta.
- **Short runs, long goals**: a run is 30–90 s; the goal (a collection, a streak, a level number) lasts weeks.

### Retention (the part we are missing)
- **Daily**: a daily challenge/puzzle with a calendar, a streak, a reward. Block Blast's "daily puzzle" framing is cited as central.
- **Progression**: levels or XP that only go up, unlocks every few sessions (new skins, new modes), a visible "next unlock" at all times.
- **Collections and customisation**: skins/themes earned by playing; "customization elements and optional upgrades" are the standard revenue points.
- **Events and social competition**: MONOPOLY GO's "event-led loops"; leaderboards among friends.
- **Loss aversion and sunk cost** used honestly: streaks you don't want to break, a collection nearly complete.

### Monetisation
- Ads-only hyper-casual nets roughly $0.03–0.08 ARPDAU and "only works at volume". Hybrid-casual layers **rewarded video as the bridge to IAP**: continue, double reward, unlock-early. Rewarded viewers are claimed to be ~6× likelier to buy.
- Do not push ads or IAP in the first sessions: "delayed, progression-linked triggers" raise ARPDAU.
- A **single IAP** that most casual players understand: *Remove ads* (plus optionally a skin bundle). Free to play stays free to play.

### UI
- Big, bottom-weighted, thumb-first. One primary action per screen. Celebration moments (confetti, bounce, haptic) on every success.
- Progress is always visible: a bar, a count, a "next at …".
- Onboarding = play. Tutorials are 1–3 beats inside the first run, never a slideshow before it.

## App Store listing
- Sizes: 1290×2796 (6.7") and 1284×2778 (6.5"); iPad optional for an iPhone-only app.
- First frame: a real screen, one idea, caption readable at thumbnail. 3–5 frames: outcome → how it works → breadth (five games) → social/competition → trust.
- US converts on minimalist, benefit-focused frames. Test treatments in App Store Connect.
- Icon: one strong shape, two colours, no text.

## Implications for Game Box (the board's brief)
1. The box needs a **meta**: XP/level across all games, a **daily challenge** with a streak and calendar, **unlockable themes/skins** per game, and a visible *next unlock*.
2. Each game needs **more juice and chain escalation** inside a run: combo multipliers, screen-wide celebrations, characters that react.
3. Tutorials should move **inside the first run** (coach marks) rather than a pre-game slideshow.
4. One honest IAP: **Remove ads**, plus rewarded video for *Continue* and *double XP*. Never interstitials.
5. Art: push every game toward a **signature palette with depth** (soft 3D shading, glow, rounded) while keeping the box's minimal chrome.
6. Store kit: icon, five+ screenshots per size with captions, keyword set, description, privacy answers, review notes.
